TOPIC: Why Indian sellers stall in the US market — and what a "US backbone" actually is.

THE GAP
An Indian seller with a good product and a working India business tries to sell in the USA and hits a wall that has nothing to do with the product:

1. No US legal entity. Marketplaces (Amazon, Walmart, Newegg, Backmarket, Etsy, eBay) want a US LLC, an EIN, an ITIN, a US bank account and marketplace approvals. Each is a separate months-long process in a foreign system.
2. Capital friction. Moving money out under LRS is slow, capped and paperwork-heavy. Working capital sits in the wrong country while inventory sits in the wrong warehouse.
3. No node on US soil. Without inventory physically inside the United States there is no Prime-speed delivery, no FBA prep, no returns handling, no QC. Cross-border shipping every order kills margin and delivery promise.
4. No compliance stack. Sales-tax registrations, IRS filings, bookkeeping, customs, duty, Section 321 — all ongoing, all US-specific, none of it optional.
5. Fragmentation. Sellers solve this with five different vendors — an incorporation agent, a freight forwarder, a 3PL, a CA, a marketplace consultant — and own all the integration risk themselves.

The result: the product was never the problem. The infrastructure was.

THE IDEA — A BACKBONE, NOT A VENDOR
A backbone is a single load-bearing spine that carries all of it: entity, capital, physical node, compliance, channel. One counterparty instead of five. The seller keeps their own brand and their own margin; the backbone carries the US-side weight.
